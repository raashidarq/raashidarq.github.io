One of the things I took away from a session on Demo Readiness and Presentation Skills was that good communication is about respecting your audience.

A few lessons stood out to me.

## Respect your audience's time

If you are given 10 minutes, you have 10 minutes. Managing your own time during a presentation is your responsibility. In professional settings, time is carefully managed, and going over your allotted time means taking away from someone else's.

## Understand why the problem matters

When presenting a project, don't just explain what you built. Understand the struggles and pain points of the people you are building for, and communicate why the problem is worth solving.

## Put yourself in the audience's shoes

Consider what they value, what they already know, and what they need from you. Good communication is tailored to the people receiving it.

## Clarity matters more than sounding impressive

Speak slowly, use short sentences, pause between key points, and avoid unnecessary jargon. Be honest and transparent, especially about limitations and things that still need work.

## Make written communication easy to follow

Use clear headings and bullet points, explain acronyms, label links clearly, and make it obvious what you want the reader or reviewer to look for.

Perhaps the biggest takeaway for me is that communication is not simply about delivering information. It is also about respecting the time, attention and perspective of the people receiving it.
